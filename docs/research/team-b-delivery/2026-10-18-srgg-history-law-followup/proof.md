# SRGG fixed-root adaptive history law: proof and boundaries

Paper source: Jia–Liu–Tang–Wang, *Constructing Efficient Identity-Based Signatures on Lattices*, local frozen PDF papers/2025_efficient_ibs_jia.pdf, SHA-256 258583c5b90255afe91343a282cb62840198b1192b6462dfc9597a24f8c01152. The scheme and original Table 4 inputs are unchanged. These are derived implementation-law statements, **not** claims made by the paper and not a security reduction.

## Adaptive coupling lemma

Let the two interactive systems have the same initial state. At each step the next operation may be selected from the entire common transcript. Suppose that, on every reachable *matching* history, the conditional next-output kernels have total variation at most delta_i, with a shared abort symbol. Couple those kernels maximally while their histories match. A divergence at step i has conditional probability at most delta_i; once diverged, charge one. Thus complete transcript TV is at most min(1, sum_i delta_i). The state, including cache, canonical byte serialization and error status, may be incorporated into the output. Common deterministic transitions add no TV cost. This does not assume independent oracle queries, but it does require fresh independent uniform random bytes for each genuinely sampled kernel under the implementation certificates.

## Fixed-root issuance-only service

Fix one of the six authenticated accepted Setup roots. The comparison is a fault-free, logically linearized FixedIssuer history under a common schedule and root/witness. The reference first-touch key kernel for canonical target h is the centered-HNF pushforward of D_(Z^N,Sigma,0) conditioned on S_h, with the **same** object/wire guard and an abort symbol. Repeated requests for a cached identity, failed/pending refusals, snapshot serialization and process restart are common deterministic transitions when histories match. Filesystem corruption, out-of-space errors, lock/timing observations, malicious rollback and arbitrary runtime faults are outside this comparison.

The predecessor [source-issuance receipt](../2026-10-18-srgg-source-issuance/README.md) replayed only Alice, but its first-touch TV *formula* is independent of the target value h: the master Schur shape is zero-centered and fixed by the same witness; the finite master bound is all-center on that plan; the gadget bound is uniform over every reachable residue/center; the digit denominator and completed-square theta bounds are uniform over centers; the fixed-root C>64I gate depends on the root, not h; and the finite successful-output coordinate envelope does not depend on h. See the predecessor [issuance-law derivation](../2026-10-18-srgg-paper-law/issuance-law.md), especially sections 3–7. identity_target either deterministically returns a canonical h in R_Q^d or raises the same error in both systems. No assumption that two identities have independent targets is used.

Consequently, for at most q first-touch identities on this **same** fixed root, the issuance-only logical-history bound is min(1,q delta_issue). Cache hits and successful restart do not increment q. The actual service byte envelope is checked separately for each profile; q cannot exceed its admitted max_users. The three standard-deviation interpretations have nontrivial per-first-touch and capacity-history bounds. Literal-density interpretations still have delta_issue=null because their gadget digit-normalizer sufficient gate did not pass; no small history bound follows. This is a same-root result and cannot be averaged over all accepted Setup roots without a bound for roots that fail the required gates.

The reference mathematical Gaussian is unbounded. It is passed through the same key-object and snapshot guard with abort, rather than pretending that every ideal sample fits the finite wire type. The predecessor proves successful **finite** samples satisfy the 256-bit coordinate guard on the audited root. The TV contraction remains valid when the common guard is applied to both sides.

## One frozen key, adaptive signing messages

The finite-chain receipt constructs the same root, extracts the same fixture key for Alice, and audits that key's signature Schur plan. This package replays that key and checks the current numerical, finite-chain and normalizer certificates byte-for-byte against the authenticated receipt. Let delta_P be that fixed key's source-Schur perturbation-to-joint-integer-Gaussian bound. Let delta_U be the current finite scalar-u batch TV including its aggregate/whole-attempt entropy cap. The challenge-dependent u centers belong to {-1,-1/2,0,1/2} for **every** binary challenge; the batch bound is uniform for adaptively chosen centers. The perturbation sampler's own hard cap is already charged in delta_P; since successful perturbation consumption is at most Pcap, an extra failure of the whole attempt source with cap Pcap+Ucap implies the u segment exceeded Ucap. The aggregate cap is therefore charged **once** in delta_U.

On each matching attempt, couple perturbations at cost delta_P, compute the same commitment and message challenge, then couple u at cost delta_U. Apply the original Algorithm 7 response, norm retry, verification, object guards and abort mapping in both worlds. For at most T=64 attempts per call and s adaptive valid messages on this **one fixed key**, transcript TV is bounded by min(1,s T min(1,delta_P+delta_U)). This includes the operational norm-exhaustion **outcome** in the comparison, but gives **no standalone upper bound on its probability** in either world. Timing and external faults are excluded. The original Algorithm 7 widths, factor one, gamma and challenge semantics remain unchanged.

## Missing bridge

The signing certificate is attached to **one fixture key**, not all keys output by the reference or actual issuance distributions. Thus the two separate results cannot be added to claim a full random-issuance-then-signature history bound. Such a claim requires an all-reachable-key Schur/finite-signing bound or a proved bad-key mass under the **same** issuance reference, plus corresponding service/fault premises. Both fields remain null. Likewise, neither an all-accepted-Setup-root law, arbitrary storage-fault law, norm-abort probability, native-strong reduction, formal hardness estimate nor NIST category follows here.

The adaptive-history lemma also does not establish the paper's SIG/IBS security games. Known same-message replacement behavior and original q/Q/2Q, zeta-factor and smoothing-unit issues remain separate.
